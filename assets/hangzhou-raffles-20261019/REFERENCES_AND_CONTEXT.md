# Hangzhou Raffles City — reference and contextual-modeling notes

Research checked 19 October 2026. This is a visually informed architectural scene, not a surveyed site model. Roads, footprints, trees, setbacks and neighboring buildings are approximate; no GIS/CAD survey was supplied.

## Confirmed architectural anchors

UNStudio describes two approximately 250 m, 60-storey towers over a six-storey retail podium, on a 40,355 m² site. The towers' silhouettes should remain complementary rather than identical. The architect's description identifies diagonally opposed tower positions, intersecting figure-eight plan logic, and a prominent southern gateway. Exterior character contrasts the podium's silver scale-like cladding with tower glazing and vertical fins. [UNStudio project](https://www.unstudio.com/projects/raffles/) and [architect-provided description and gallery, ArchDaily](https://www.archdaily.com/879869/raffles-city-hangzhou-unstudio).

The owner confirms the address as 228 Xinye Road and identifies the nearby Civic Centre, Grand Theatre and International Conference Centre. [CapitaLand](https://www.capitaland.com/cn/zh/lease/offices-listing/rafflescityhangzhou-copy-copy-copy.html).

## Geographic relationships and modeling choices

- Raffles City belongs to a dense urban block inland from the Qiantang River. It should not look like an island or an isolated waterfront resort.
- The river is broadly southeast of the complex. It is beyond other city/cultural/park parcels, rather than directly against the mall. For a compact visual scene, a shoreline setback of approximately 750–1,000 m from the tower centre is a reasonable artistic assumption, NOT a measured distance.
- Approximate public-map coordinates: Raffles City 30.25078° N, 120.20850° E; Grand Theatre 30.24662° N, 120.21070° E. This puts the theatre about 510 m south-southeast of Raffles City, already toward the waterfront. Sources: [Raffles City map coordinate record](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Q11102554), [Grand Theatre OpenStreetMap-derived record](https://mapcarta.com/W626838819). Coordinate precision does not imply survey accuracy; do not mix Chinese GCJ-02/BD-09 and WGS84 data without conversion.
- Local streets form a rotated grid. Fuchun Road runs roughly northeast–southwest at the site's southeast / river-facing edge. Xinye Road is the southwest cross-street, trending northwest–southeast. Dangui Street is at the northeast edge and Minxin Road at the northwest edge. Common Chinese descriptions simplify these as south, west, east and north respectively. [Street boundary summary](https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/杭州来福士中心); [Amap listing at Fuchun / Dangui](https://www.amap.com/place/B0H07X5CU0). Treat road orientation and widths in this scene as approximate.
- Civic Centre sits diagonally across the street toward the southwest. The public/cultural landscape continues toward the river. In broad context, use a civic plaza/green axis, a low sculptural theatre volume, and a separate golden rounded conference/hotel landmark, keeping these subordinate to the twin towers.
- Qiantang should read as a very broad river with a distant opposite bank, roughly parallel to the northeast–southwest street grain. A narrow ornamental watercourse visible beyond a perimeter boulevard in historical aerial photography is a separate feature and must not be mistaken for Qiantang.
- Nearby massing should be mostly rectilinear modern office towers on podiums, with varied heights and deliberate street setbacks. Exact neighboring footprints, facade details and present-day construction status were not reconstructed.

## Waterfront reference

The City Balcony is an elevated riverfront public landscape, not the Raffles podium itself. The landscape designer documents a 5.5 ha project completed in 2006. [POLA City Balcony](https://www.pola-berlin.de/en/projects/city-balcony). This supports using an embankment promenade, broad public platform and landscaped river approach, with artistic simplification.

## Owner-reviewed refinement: atrium and opposite bank, 19 October 2026

The owner rejected the old central black opening in the podium roof. The official UNStudio description calls the podium's spiral atrium a central organizing space. The official photograph `axo124_n28_wwwkits.jpg` visibly shows an overhead glazed roof on a steel grid with light-colored multi-level galleries beneath. The new modeled skylight follows the existing opening boundary with an orthogonal steel grid and glazed surface; internal light lining, a floor and gallery ledges replace the unmodeled dark shaft. The photograph establishes the construction type, not this model's exact roof coordinates, member spacing or internal floor heights.

Official photo: https://a.storyblok.com/f/324448/3000x3267/35a8175af8/axo124_n28_wwwkits.jpg
Official project text: https://www.unstudio.com/projects/raffles/

For the opposite bank, Hangzhou's official city portal documents the urban growth along both Qiantang banks and the Binjiang waterfront. Added layered city blocks, a waterfront greenbelt, promenade and roads are inspired by those broad landscape characteristics. No exact identification of each distant tower is claimed.
